Flowy is built around one connected canvas where every idea is a node you can generate, connect, and refine. Once you understand a few core ideas, the rest of Flowy clicks into place. This page walks you through them.

## The infinite canvas

The canvas is your primary workspace, and it never runs out of room. You work at the speed of thought here: ideate, iterate, and refine without leaving the page. Drop in prompts, pan across your ideas, and zoom out to see the whole story at once.

## Nodes and connections

A node is the basic unit of the canvas. It is either a piece of content, like **Text**, **Image**, **Video**, or **Audio**, or a generator that produces new content from a prompt. You connect a node's output into another node's input to pass results forward, so one image can feed a video, and that video can feed a composition.

When you want to explore options, add a **Router** to fan out into variations and fan them back in. Use a **Group** to organize related nodes, and drop a **Sticky note** to annotate your thinking.

<Tip>
  Connections are how context flows. Wire nodes together and each step builds on the last.
</Tip>

## Canvas vs Studio

Flowy has two editors that work together. **Canvas** is the node graph where you generate assets and branch through ideas. **Studio** is the timeline editor where you compose those assets into a finished, exportable video.

You switch between them anytime with the mode-switch pill. A composition you create on the canvas opens directly in **Studio**, so your work stays in continuity from frame to film.

## Generations and credits

Generating content consumes credits from your wallet. The lifecycle is simple: credits are first *reserved* when a generation starts, then *deducted* on success, or *refunded* automatically if it fails. New accounts start with free credits so you can explore right away.

<Note>
  You never lose credits for a failed generation. Refunds happen automatically. See [Credits](/billing/credits) for the full picture.
</Note>

## Workspaces and projects

A workspace holds your projects and your team members. A project lives inside a workspace and holds your canvas. You can create or switch workspaces, invite people, and keep related work together in one place.
